Language prediction can reduce explicit selections in a brain-computer interface only when an intended continuation appears in the displayed candidate set. We developed NeuroSelect, a reproducible offline framework that separates candidate generation, profile-conditioned ranking, P300 decoding, retrieval, fusion, and confirmation. Four synthetic profiles supplied 3,990 held-out next-span trials. Public, deidentified Study P data were split by participant for original-task target/non-target flash decoding, and recorded probabilities were remapped in a counterfactual candidate replay. The frozen language generator made the intended span available in 28.7% of trials, with 0.0% complete-message availability. Conditional on availability, personalization improved top-1 recall by 9.6 percentage points overall but reduced it by 9.1 points for the concise profile. On 21,491 held-out epochs, calibrated xDAWN-LDA achieved AUROC 0.800 and Brier score 0.062; EEGNet showed no established selection-ranking advantage and worse calibration. Counterfactual full fusion changed top-1 recall by 0.7 percentage points and completion by 0.0 points relative to BCI-only replay. Exploratory target-blind generation and compositional openings improved some coverage measures but failed on unseen paraphrase families. These offline results identify candidate coverage, especially message openings, as the main observed bottleneck. They do not demonstrate live communication benefit, clinical efficacy, participant intent, or thought decoding.
